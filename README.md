# THE EXIT INTERVIEW
> Confidential: do not discuss.

Five years ago, Jason was good at his job. He still is. That is the whole problem.

He closed deals for Gunman Corp. These were the kind of deals that do not show up on paper, made for buyers who should not exist: banned regimes, private militias, and anyone with enough money and few enough questions. He told himself it was above his pay grade to ask. Then he told himself the commission made it not his problem. Then he stopped telling himself anything at all and just got good at it.

Nine deals later, he is resigning. Not out of conscience, but more like the slow accumulation of weight a person can only carry so far before they have to put it down somewhere.

Gunman Corp does not do exit interviews out of courtesy.

Across the table sits Noman, Head of Corporate Security. He is not here to find out what Jason did because he already knows. He is here to find out what it is going to cost to keep Jason quiet, and whether Jason is smart enough to take the deal or foolish enough to make this difficult.

Every answer is a negotiation with two currencies: how much Jason seems willing to just disappear, and how much he seems to be holding in reserve. Play too compliant, and he leaves with nothing but his silence. Play too sharp, and Noman starts wondering if Jason is worth the risk of leaving alive to talk.

Somewhere underneath the interview room, in flashback, is the story of how a man who used to ask questions stopped asking them, one reasonable-sounding compromise at a time.

There is no good ending here. There is only the one you can live with.

You are the bad guy.  
The only question is what kind.
